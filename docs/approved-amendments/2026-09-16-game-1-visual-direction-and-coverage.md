## Approved court-board direction — separate visual system

Build a dynamic, dimensionally accurate, directly overhead basketball court, retaining the approved timber appearance, navy surrounds, verified Hawthorn Magic centre-court logo asset, and clear numbered player markers with Charlotte distinguished. Court-board diagram style is intentionally distinct from the locked narrative illustration style. ### Charlotte EDJBA U10 ruleset — locked profile basis

When Charlotte's profile selects **EDJBA** and the relevant U10 age group, the presentation/rules context uses the current EDJBA U10-U11 rules: size 5 ball; foul line advanced 1 metre; three-pointers included using the inner three-point line; five seconds in the key; zone defence not allowed. The underlying court geometry uses the Basketball Victoria/FIBA-standard 28m x 15m court. For the inner/junior three-point presentation, use the applicable Victorian junior arc geometry (6.25m radius) and verify the exact implementation against the current competition/facility standard before release. Rules/configuration must be versioned rather than assumed immutable because competition rules can change.

The no-zone rule means idealised defensive positioning should teach age-appropriate player-oriented/man-to-man principles: relationship to player and basket, awareness of ball and player, help/recover, matching up and protecting dangerous space, not fixed zone occupation.

### U10 positioning model — approved product behaviour

For each court state, all ten on-court players should occupy believable positions that make the basketball situation understandable. Magic offence should create useful spacing, passing lanes and opportunities rather than clustering around the ball. Magic defence should generally show players recovering between opponents and basket, matching up, protecting dangerous space and responding to the ball. Transition offence should show players advancing in useful lanes and providing passing options; transition defence should show players getting back, protecting the basket and finding opponents. Shot and rebound states should move relevant players into plausible rebounding, outlet or defensive-transition positions. U10 play remains imperfect and believable: players may become crowded, late, beaten or too wide where the situation warrants it.

### Pre-choice truth / post-choice movement — locked

At a decision, the court shows **the problem Charlotte actually faces before she chooses**. It must not move Charlotte into an ideal answer, reveal the consequence, or visually coach the selected response in advance. After selection, Charlotte and relevant players may transition to coordinates consistent with the action and resolved event. If the choice leaves her in a weaker position, the court may honestly show that too.

### Sideline/front-court spacing — approved new playable learning situation

A recurring U10 pattern is approved for occasional Game 1 inclusion, subject to product/content integration rather than silent insertion into certified mechanics. In transition Charlotte or another player may initially run wide near the sideline. As the attack crosses approximately half court and develops into the front court, remaining pinned to the boundary can reduce attacking angles, narrow passing options and carry the player toward the corner. Moving away from the boundary into useful space can create a better passing lane and room to attack, shoot or drive.

This can become a genuine Charlotte decision. Before the choice, show the authentic problem. Only after the choice animate the selected movement and resolve a plausible consequence. Better spacing must not guarantee a pass or score. Avoid hard-coded coaching jargon unless separately approved as natural dialogue.

### Off-ball availability — approved learning behaviour

When Magic have the ball and Charlotte is off-ball, her location matters. Give genuine opportunities to recognise useful space, become available for a pass and, where appropriate, become ready to attack or shoot. This extends existing authored concepts of calling for the ball, moving into open space and keeping moving. A strong off-ball choice may improve visibility, passing angle or scoring opportunity without guaranteeing a pass. Off-ball contribution remains meaningful even if Charlotte never touches the ball.

### Fast break / transition attack — approved first-class situation family

Fast break is a first-class Game 1 situation family, not merely an illustration. It may emerge naturally when Magic gain possession from a defensive rebound, Charlotte or teammate steal, loose-ball recovery, opponent turnover or another truthful possession-change event. It must not trigger automatically after every possession gain: the presentation/content state must support a plausible transition opportunity.

If Charlotte controls the ball in the defensive end with transition available, the situation can ask her to recognise options such as pushing the ball up court, passing ahead to an open teammate, or controlling the possession when appropriate. Exact choice copy and probabilities remain content-authority work and must be tested before implementation. The court must show defender recovery, teammate lanes and actual space so the decision is spatially meaningful.

If a teammate controls the ball, Charlotte may receive an off-ball transition decision such as sprinting into useful space, running an appropriate wide lane or becoming a passing option. A good choice can create an opportunity but never guarantees Charlotte receives the ball or scores. Transition can chain into a receiving, ball-hand, shooting, sideline/front-court-spacing or half-court state. Fast-break outcomes include plausible continuation, pass, layup/shot opportunity, foul, miss, turnover or settled half-court possession according to engine/content authority.

Early transition width and front-court spacing must coexist: running wide can be useful early, while staying pinned to the boundary as the attack develops can become the approved sideline-spacing problem.

## Team identity, profile and belonging — locked product direction

The game should feel like Charlotte is one player within **her basketball world**. Profile setup must never block immediate play.

### Competition and club

Charlotte may optionally identify her competition as **EDJBA** and her club as **Hawthorn Magic**. When EDJBA is selected, opposition club identity should be drawn from the current real EDJBA member-club universe, excluding Charlotte's own club, rather than a permanently invented opponent such as Falcons. The EDJBA club dataset must be maintained as versioned configuration because membership/naming can change. Use verified EDJBA-published club names and, where useful, published uniform colours. Do not invent/redraw official logos or imply affiliation/endorsement.

### Magic squad

Charlotte's squad supports **Charlotte plus up to eight teammates** (up to nine players total). Teammate entry is completely optional and lightweight. Charlotte may enter teammates' first names; jersey numbers are optional. She can add/edit them later. Missing squad identities are filled with fictional age-appropriate players so she can press Play immediately. Partial personalisation is supported: entered teammates coexist with generated teammates.

Exactly five players are represented on court at a time. Remaining squad members are bench players. Background substitutions may rotate players naturally but are not Charlotte decisions in Game 1. Narrative, roster and court must reference only players who are actually on court for an on-court action. A roster treatment may show the full squad while clearly distinguishing on-court and bench players.

Custom teammate names are dynamic profile data and must **not** be baked into narrative artwork. Artwork depicts basketball roles/situations; UI/narrative supplies dynamic names and numbers. Current prototype names Mia/Zoe/Avery/Harper are not permanent canon.

### Opposition

Use real EDJBA club identities but **fictional child/player identities**. Do not fabricate a child and imply they are a real player for an EDJBA club. Opposition can have a believable squad larger than five with exactly five on court. Names/numbers may be generated and must remain internally consistent within a game. Current hard-coded Falcons/Casey/Taylor/Morgan/Riley/Jordan presentation casting is not permanent canon. Existing `contest.png` remains usable only where its visual/event truth is compatible; its legacy metadata must not force the runtime opponent to be called Falcons.

On smaller screens roster UI may adapt/collapse, but team-belonging should be preserved. Charlotte must remain easy to identify.

## Approved experience sequence

Court context → illustrated decision moment → genuine choice → engine-matched illustrated consequence where meaningful → updated court context. Context artwork contains only decision-time information. Outcome artwork/copy matches the recorded event, possession, score and chronology. The post-game story follows `2026-09-16-every-game-tells-your-story.md`.

Preserve the three originals. The initial production target is **13 new reusable narrative scenes**, with the dynamic court carrying ordinary movement, possession changes and spatial transitions. Asset count is a ceiling for this package, not a requirement to create redundant variants.

1. `offball-space-decision` — Charlotte off-ball with defender relationship and useful space visible.
2. `sideline-space-decision` — Charlotte too close to the boundary as transition enters the front court.
3. `ballhand-pressure-decision` — Charlotte has the ball with defender pressure and credible attack/pass options.
4. `shooting-opportunity-decision` — genuine shot/drive/pump-fake opportunity before outcome.
5. `charlotte-attack` — Charlotte attacks the basket/gap after the relevant selected action.
6. `charlotte-score` — resolved earned scoring moment; must match recorded shot/action truth.
7. `onball-defence-decision` — opponent attacks Charlotte with the ball.
8. `shot-defence-decision` — opponent visibly preparing a two or declared three; composition/variant must preserve shot-value truth.
9. `charlotte-steal` — Charlotte wins possession defensively.
10. `rebound-decision` — ball genuinely live after a miss before winner is known.
11. `charlotte-rebound` — use existing `rebound.png` where truthful; do not recreate merely to hit asset count. If counted as existing coverage, the new-art count is correspondingly lower.
12. `defensive-recovery-decision` — Charlotte recovering in transition after possession is lost.
13. `fast-break-transition-opportunity` — Charlotte pushing the ball or sprinting into a real transition opportunity; pre-outcome and reusable only where possession/role truth matches.

Existing `contest.png` covers only its truthful contested-opponent-make use case. Existing `rebound.png` covers Charlotte securing a rebound. Existing `charlotte.png` remains support/portrait artwork.

Free throws remain fully supported gameplay and regression requirements; this decision concerns bespoke art only.

Before generation, each new scene receives a scene brief with pre-choice/outcome classification, ball ownership, court orientation, required/forbidden visual facts, character-role casting, reuse limits and engine/event mappings. Obtain Jared's explicit art approval before integration.
