### U10 positioning model — approved product behaviour

The board is not a literal player-tracking system. It is a **realistic but developmentally idealised U10 basketball representation** of the current authored/engine situation. The engine remains authoritative for score, clock, possession, ball state and recorded outcomes; a presentation positioning model may determine believable player coordinates where the engine does not track all ten players. Such coordinates must be labelled/tested as presentation state and must never be represented as engine-observed physical locations.

For each court state, all ten players should occupy believable positions that make the basketball situation understandable. Magic offence should create useful spacing, passing lanes and opportunities rather than five players clustering around the ball. Magic defence should generally show players recovering between opponents and the basket, matching up, protecting dangerous space and responding to the ball. Transition offence should show players advancing into useful lanes and providing passing options; transition defence should show players getting back, protecting the basket and finding opponents. Shot and rebound states should move relevant players into plausible rebounding, outlet or defensive-transition positions. U10 play must remain believable rather than appearing like perfectly executed professional basketball: players can become crowded, late, beaten, too wide or otherwise imperfect when the situation or previous choice warrants it.

### Pre-choice truth / post-choice movement — locked

At a decision, the court shows **the problem Charlotte actually faces before she chooses**. It must not move Charlotte into an ideal answer, reveal the consequence, or visually coach the selected response in advance. After her selection, Charlotte and relevant players may transition to coordinates consistent with that action and the resolved basketball event. If the choice leaves her in a weaker position, the court may honestly show that too. The movement itself should help Charlotte understand the spatial consequence without requiring a lecture.

### Sideline-lane transition situation — approved new playable learning situation

A recurring real U10 pattern is approved for inclusion as an occasional Game 1 situation, subject to normal product-authority integration rather than silent insertion into certified mechanics. In transition, Charlotte or another player may initially run very wide near the sideline/boundary. As the attack crosses approximately half court and develops into the front court, remaining pinned to the boundary can reduce attacking angles, narrow passing options and carry the player toward the corner. Moving away from the sideline into useful space can create a better passing lane and more room to attack, shoot or drive.

This should sometimes become a genuine Charlotte decision rather than merely an automatic positioning correction. Before the choice, show Charlotte authentically near the sideline with the relevant teammates, defenders and ball state. Offer age-appropriate choices derived from the actual situation; do not label one as correct or use adult coaching jargon. Only after the choice should the board animate her selected movement and the game resolve a plausible consequence. The better-spaced action must not guarantee Charlotte receives the ball or scores. The lesson is recognition and use of space, not a deterministic reward. Avoid hard-coding a coach shout such as 'get out of the lane' as an answer prompt unless separately approved as natural story dialogue.

### Off-ball availability and shot-ready space — approved learning behaviour

When Magic have the ball and Charlotte is off-ball, her location should matter. Game 1 should give her genuine opportunities to recognise useful space where she can become available for a pass and, where appropriate, be ready to attack or shoot. This extends the existing authored concepts of calling for the ball, moving into open space and keeping moving; it does not replace them.

The board should make the spatial problem legible: Charlotte may be hidden behind a defender, too close to a teammate, standing in occupied space, or have an open passing lane available. Choices can include calling, moving to useful open space, continuing movement or another contextually authentic option already permitted by the game contract. The board must not pre-position Charlotte in the ideal receiving spot before she chooses. A strong off-ball choice may improve teammate visibility, passing angle or scoring opportunity without guaranteeing a pass. Off-ball contribution remains meaningful even if Charlotte never touches the ball.

## Team identity and belonging layer — approved

The game should visibly feel like **Charlotte is one player within a real team playing another team**. Show the five current Hawthorn Magic players and five current opposition players on the court, with stable presentation identities. Around the court, provide a readable roster treatment for both sides: Hawthorn Magic on one side and the opposition on the other, each showing the five players currently represented on court. Charlotte should be clearly identifiable within Magic. The ball carrier or other currently relevant player may be subtly indicated when useful, but roster UI must not imply unsupported statistics or tactical status.

Player names and numbers must correspond consistently between roster, court markers, narrative illustrations where named, and presentation casting. Existing invented presentation names are not automatically approved as permanent canon merely because they appear in a prototype; audit them before implementation. The opposition team name must likewise come from the approved Game 1 source/product decision and remain consistent throughout the interface. Do not invent official club branding. On smaller screens the roster treatment may collapse or adapt, but the team-belonging function should be preserved rather than simply removed; test the mobile solution with Charlotte for comprehension and distraction.
